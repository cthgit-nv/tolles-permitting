OPINION BY ADMINISTRATIVE JUDGE SOSIN 
 
Owyhee County appealed four decisions issued by four different local offices of the 
Bureau of Land Management (BLM) in Idaho. In each decision, BLM approved a Travel 
Management Plan (TMP) for BLM-managed lands in Owyhee County, Idaho, designating 
travel routes for motorized and mechanized off-highway vehicle (OHV) recreation. In this 
Decision, we grant BLM’s motion to dismiss Owyhee County’s appeals because the County 
has not demonstrated standing to appeal the decisions. 
 
SUMMARY 
 
An appellant has the burden to demonstrate that it has standing to appeal a 
decision to this Board by showing that it is both a party to a case and adversely affected 
by the decision. To be adversely affected, the appellant must have a legally cognizable 
interest that the decision is substantially likely to injure. Owyhee County claims that it is 
adversely affected by the TMPs because BLM failed to consider some of the County’s 
asserted Revised Statute (R.S.) 2477 rights-of-way. That omission, the County argues, 
will leave it subject to adverse actions by BLM for unauthorized use and maintenance of 
those asserted rights-of-way, will impair the County’s ability to provide government 
services to its citizens, and violates the Omnibus Public Land Management Act and the 
National Environmental Policy Act. The County’s assertions of adverse effects from 
potential actions by BLM and to its ability to provide government services are speculative 
or vague and unsupported, and therefore, they are insufficient to demonstrate any likely 
injury to a legally cognizable interest. Nor is the County’s assertion of injury from BLM’s

ANALYSIS 
 
I. The Standing Requirements 
 
The Department’s regulations provide that to have standing to appeal to this 
Board, an appellant must show that it is both a “party to a case” and “adversely affected” 
by the decision it seeks to appeal.49 An appellant bears the burden to demonstrate that it 
satisfies both elements,50 and if it does not, the Board must dismiss the appeal for lack of 
jurisdiction.51 
 
An appellant is a “party to a case” when it has “taken action that is the subject of 
the decision on appeal, is the object of that decision, or has otherwise participated in the 
process leading to the decision under appeal.”52 A party to a case is “adversely affected” 
by a decision “when that party has a legally cognizable interest, and the decision on 
appeal has caused or is substantially likely to cause injury to that interest.”53 
 
A legally cognizable interest may be an economic or property interest in the 
affected public lands or adjacent land or a cultural, recreational, or aesthetic use and 
enjoyment of those lands.54 A mere interest in a problem or concern about the issues 
involved is not a legally cognizable interest.55 Similarly, an appellant cannot show 
standing by simply alleging that an agency violated the law.56 Rather, an appellant must 
show that the alleged violation of the law injured or is substantially likely to injure the 
appellant’s legally cognizable interest separate and apart from procedural injury.57

To establish that a decision is substantially likely to injure a legally cognizable 
interest, an appellant must make colorable allegations, supported by specific facts, that 
establish a causal relationship between the decision and the alleged injury.58 An appellant 
need not prove that an injury is certain, but it must show that the threat of injury and its 
effect on the appellant are more than hypothetical.59 
 
Here, because Owyhee County participated in the process leading to the decisions 
on appeal by commenting on the EAs, it is a party to a case.60 We therefore focus our 
analysis on whether the County has demonstrated that it is adversely affected by BLM’s 
decisions. 
 
II. Owyhee County Lacks Standing to Appeal the TMPs 
 
The County advances three arguments for how it is adversely affected by the 
omission of certain of its asserted R.S. 2477 rights-of-way from BLM’s inventory 
process.61 For purposes of our analysis, we assume the County has sufficiently identified 
legally cognizable interests implicated by the TMPs. But as explained below, we conclude 
that the County’s assertions of adverse effect are insufficient to show a substantial 
likelihood of injury to a legally cognizable interest, and therefore, the County lacks 
standing. 
 
a. The County’s allegations of injury from potential BLM actions against the 
County do not show any adverse effect from the TMPs 
 
First, the County states that BLM’s omission from its inventory process of the 
County’s asserted R.S. 2477 rights-of-way means that BLM “does not authorize any ‘use’ 
of such omitted roads and/or any ‘maintenance’ of such omitted road,” which, in turn, 
means that “any ‘use or maintenance’ by Owyhee County on these omitted routes” will 
expose the County to adverse actions by BLM.62 The County argues, therefore, that it 
“will be subject to claims of unauthorized use, whether civilly or criminally, by BLM.”63

c. The County’s allegations of injury from BLM’s procedural violations do not 
show any adverse effect from the TMPs 
  
Finally, although unclear, Owyhee County may be suggesting that BLM’s omission 
of some of the County’s asserted R.S. 2477 rights-of-way constitutes procedural 
violations of OPLMA and NEPA that alone satisfy the requirement to demonstrate 
adverse effects from BLM’s TMPs.80 The County states that BLM’s omission of certain 
asserted R.S. 2477 rights-of-way from the route inventory that informed the TMPs 
resulted in “several material, adverse consequences,” including “a lack of conformance to 
BLM’s statutory obligation[s],” under the OPLMA and NEPA.81 But procedural injuries, 
alone, cannot provide an independent basis for standing. Under the Board’s precedent, to 
create standing, a procedural injury must be related to an injury to a legally cognizable 
interest apart from the alleged procedural violation.82 Because Owyhee County has not 
otherwise shown an injury to a legally cognizable interest, it cannot show standing based 
on alleged violations of the OPLMA and NEPA alone. 
  
CONCLUSION 
 
 Owyhee County has not shown that BLM’s TMPs are substantially likely to injure 
its legally cognizable interests. The County’s claim that it will be subject to adverse 
actions by BLM based on the County’s use and maintenance of omitted R.S. 2477 rights-
of-way is speculative, and its claim that BLM’s TMPs will adversely affect the County’s 
ability to provide various government services lacks particularity. These claims, 
therefore, are insufficient to establish standing. The County’s asserted procedural injuries 
from BLM’s alleged failure to comply with the OPLMA and NEPA are not sufficient to 
support standing in the absence of injury to a separate legally cognizable interest. 
Because Owyhee County has not demonstrated that its legally cognizable interests are 
substantially likely to be injured by BLM’s decisions approving the TMPs, it does not have
