A. DESCRIPTION OF THE NEW PROPOSED ACTION
(Note that the description below is based on information provided to the BLM in the Applicant’s 
plan of development.) 
Townsite Solar 2 LLC (Applicant) currently holds authorization to construct, operate, and 
maintain a 19 MW solar project plus a 35 MW Battery Storage System (BESS) pursuant to 
Bureau of Land Management (BLM) right of way (ROW) Grant N-99875 issued on September 
22, 2023. The Applicant proposes to modify the ROW grant as outlined in the amended SF-299 
submitted on November 4, 2025 and further updated on January 8, 2026 and in their updated 
plan of development (POD) dated May 2026. 
The Applicant now proposes to construct, own, operate, maintain, and eventually decommission 
a data center on 81 acres of the existing 85-acre ROW area, with an additional 4 acres to be used 
for an 80 × 2,000–foot linear facilities segment (Project / new proposed action).  
The Project consists of the data center building housing computer servers, storage, and network 
equipment to store, manage, process, and disseminate large amounts of data and digital 
information, and administrative facilities. Contiguous to the data center building will be surface 
parking, a 70 MW battery energy storage system (BESS), an electrical substation, and 
emergency backup generators fueled by biodiesel.  
[[page 2]]
In addition, the linear facilities portion of the Project will contain a road to access the data center 
site (and temporary laydown area) during construction and operation, installation of a permanent 
12- to 13.8-kilovolt (kV) electrical distribution powerline (distribution line), and an underground 
34.5-kV to 230 kV power collection system used to interconnect the Project into a new 
substation proposed to support the expansion of the Southern Nevada Water Authority (SNWA) 
230-kV transmission line (N-100382). The linear facilities will connect to the southeast corner of 
the data center and run due east for approximately 2,000 feet to the western border of the project 
being developed by the Applicant on municipal land owned by the City of Boulder City (BCNV 
Property). The Project will interconnect into the newly expanded SNWA 230-kV transmission 
line or into the existing Western Area Power Administration (WAPA) Mead 230-kV Substation 
from the existing Townsite Solar 1 facilities, both located on municipal land. 
Site access will use two separate locations from U.S. Highway 95. The first point of site access is 
located approximately 1.24 miles south of the Interstate Highway 11 (I-11) / U.S. Highway 93 
interchange with U.S. Highway 95, then west onto non-BLM lands using an unnamed road (the 
road to access Impact Sand and Gravel’s Boulder Ranch Quarry (BRQ Pit)) for 0.25 mile, then 
north on Old U.S. Highway 95 (Old Hwy 95) for 0.35 mile to the proposed Project’s linear 
facilities segment on BLM land. The second point of site access is a right in / right out only point 
of access located approximately 0.9 miles south of I-11 / U.S. Highway 93 interchange with U.S. 
Highway 95. If needed, the Applicant will obtain authorization from the governing jurisdictions 
for use of non-BLM roads for access.  
To minimize potential environmental impacts, the proposed project area is slightly larger than 
required to allow for adjustments in the facility layout. Any land ultimately not needed for the 
Project will be relinquished back to the BLM at the appropriate time. 
Below is a brief description of the Project’s proposed elements:  
• Geotechnical investigation work includes soil borings, soil resistivity testing, pile load 
testing, and access. Up to six exploratory test borings to depths of approximately 30 and 
50 feet, two field soil resistivity tests (Wenner four-pin method) along two perpendicular 
traverses at each borehole, and pile load testing at four locations with a group of three 
piles installed at target depths of 7 and 13 feet. Underground Service Alert will locate and 
mark the horizontal and vertical locations of exiting utilities prior to boring. Access will 
be by existing trails and overland travel using a truck-mounted drill rig and conventional 
pickup truck(s) or four-wheel drive truck(s). All disturbance will be restricted to the 
Project Area. 
• Construction Laydown area: Located adjacent to and outside of the southeast corner of 
the Project Area, measuring approximately 467 × 464 and totaling 5 acres. This acreage 
will initially be used as the laydown area and will subsequently be the location of a 70 
MW BESS and switching station.  
• Data center facility footprint:  
o Current GIS datasets provided by the Applicant show a total of four data center 
buildings, each with a rectangular footprint area of approximately 300,000 square 
feet. All four buildings are proposed for development within the footprint of the 
previously authorized solar facility analyzed in the existing NEPA document. 
o The exact dimensions are not fixed, as they depend on factors like server density, 
cooling systems, and overall building design. The data center building will not 
exceed 25 feet in height.  
[[page 3]]
o Permanent fencing will be installed around the perimeter of the data center. The 
perimeter fencing will include a 6- or 7-foot-tall chain-link fence with 1-foot-high 
barbed-wire security strands along the top. Installation of permanent Mojave 
desert tortoise (Gopherus agassizii) fencing will follow U.S. Fish and Wildlife 
Service (USFWS) guidelines and will be attached to the bottom of the perimeter 
fencing. Both will be installed concurrently. 
o One auxiliary voltage transformer, measuring 61 × 62 × 71 inches mounted on a 
concrete pad adjacent to and connected to the distribution line. 
o Installation of a BESS and appurtenant systems will provide up to 70 MW, 4- to 
8-hour capacity system for the electric grid.  
o Installation of an electric switching station within the 5 acres to be initially used 
as an equipment laydown area during construction.  
o Installation of 167 MW of back-up biodiesel-fired generators.  
o Underground Power Collection Line alternating current (AC) collection system 
will transfer power from the DC collection system and PCSs and transfer the 
power through the underground Power Collection Line to the Linear Facilities 
segment where the electricity will interconnect into the grid.  
• The Linear Facilities segment extending along the southern border of the project footprint 
to the east, measuring 80 feet wide × 2,000 feet long, ending at the western border of the 
proposed BCNV Property, on municipal land owned by the City of Boulder City, and will 
be used for the site road, auxiliary distribution line, underground Power Collection Line, 
and fiber optic cable: 
o Site road: The proposed Project will use the 30-foot-wide × 2,000-foot-long 
portion of the linear facilities segment for the main access to the data center 
facility, which will provide site access during construction and operation of the 
Project. The site road will be graded.  
o Auxiliary overhead distribution line: The remaining 50-foot-wide × 2,000-foot-
long portion of the linear facilities segment will be used for an overhead 12- to 
13.8-kV auxiliary distribution line to provide power during construction and 
operations. The Applicant intends to assign the 50 × 2,000-foot portion of the 
linear facilities segment to the owner of the distribution line at a later date. 
o Interconnection and communications: The 50-foot-wide × 2,000-foot-long portion 
of the linear facilities segment will also be shared for the construction and 
operation of the underground Power Collection Line and fiber optic cabling. The 
underground 34.5-230 kV collection line will deliver electricity into the on-site 
BESS or to the new SNWA substation in support of the newly expanded SNWA 
230-kV transmission line. The Power Collection Line will require two pulling 
sites, up to 80 × 300 feet each, to be within a portion of the linear facilities 
segment (80 × 2,000-foot area) for an approximate two-month construction 
timeframe. Fiber optic cabling will be installed either underground with the Power 
Connection Line as a separate conduit or on overhead lines along the distribution 
line.  
• Project security using a combination of perimeter fencing around the data center 
buildings, BESS and switching station, controlled access gates, on-site security patrols, 
lighting, electronic security systems and/or remote monitoring. 
[[page 4]]
• Temporary fencing: Desert tortoise exclusion fencing (temporary) will be installed 
around the linear facilities segment (4 acres) to reduce the number of biological monitors 
required on-site. At least two desert tortoise gates will be installed within the linear 
facilities segment and one entrance gate to the data center facility (with permanent desert 
tortoise fencing attached). The desert tortoise fencing will be installed concurrently with 
the perimeter fencing.  
Non-potable reclaimed water will be obtained from the City of Boulder City Wastewater 
Treatment Plant through an existing pipeline for dust suppression activities and stored on-site in 
aboveground water tanks at the temporary laydown area. If water from the City of Boulder City 
Wastewater Treatment Plant is not available, water would be obtained from commercial sources 
with existing water rights or availability within a reasonable distance of the project area. This 
water, if needed, would be trucked to the project area.  
The Project will be designed in accordance with federal and industrial standards, including 
American Society of Mechanical Engineers standards, the National Electrical Code (National 
Fire Protection Association [NFPA] 2008), the International Building Code (International Code 
Council 2006), the International Energy Conservation Code (International Code Council 2006), 
the Uniform Plumbing Code (International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials 
2006), the Uniform Mechanical Code (International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical 
Officials 2006), NFPA standards, and Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) 
standards. 
Construction is expected to take up to 18 months and will include mobilization, construction 
grading and site preparation, installation of drainage and erosion controls, data center and BESS 
construction.

D. NEPA ADEQUACY CRITERIA
1. Is the new proposed action a feature of, or substantially the same as an alternative
analyzed in the existing environmental document(s)?
Yes, the new proposed action (data center) is essentially like the original 2023 proposed action 
(photovoltaic solar energy facility) analyzed in the existing 2023 Environmental Assessment. 
Both proposals are from LLC applicants operated by the same parent corporate entity. Both 
proposals will use the exact same location, same acreage, and same perimeter. Both are 
proposals for industrial uses that will operationalize cutting-edge technologies that are 
predominantly electrical and solid-state in nature. Both proposals are connected to external 
electrical infrastructure via the same proposed utility corridor. Both proposals will use the same 
access roads and construction laydown footprints. Both involve improvements/facilities that 
are visually geometric and less than 30 feet in height. Both propose grading the project site in 
order to accommodate development. Both proposals would require fencing of the site perimeter 
to ensure site security.  Both proposals involve construction timeframes of 12-18 months using 
similar equipment and vehicles. 
A description of the original 2023 proposed action is presented in the existing BLM NEPA 
document DOI-BLM-NV-S010-2023-0016-EA on pages 4 - 11.  
2. Is the proposed action within the same analysis area, or if the location of the proposed
action is different, are the geographic and resource conditions sufficiently similar to
those analyzed in the existing environmental document(s)? If there are differences, can
you explain why they are not substantial?
Yes, the new proposed action is within the exact same analysis area as that which was analyzed 
in the existing 2023 Environmental Assessment. Location, acreage, and perimeter shape and size 
are all the same. 
A description of the analysis area for the original 2023 proposed action is presented in the 
existing BLM NEPA document DOI-BLM-NV-S010-2023-0016-EA in Appendix A – Table A-2 
and Figures A-1 through A-4. 
[[page 6]]
3. Is the range of alternatives analyzed in the existing environmental document(s) 
appropriate with respect to the new proposed action, given current environmental 
concerns and resource values? 
Yes, the range of alternatives analyzed in the 2023 Environmental Assessment is appropriate 
with respect to the new proposed action.  There are no known environmental concerns or 
resource values which would mandate the addition of new action alternatives beyond the 
alternatives that were analyzed in the existing 2023 Environmental Assessment: the original 
2023 proposed action and the “no action” alternative.  
Any action alternative [1] must satisfy the Purpose and Need statement (which – for applicant-
driven proposals – typically is for BLM to respond to the application), [2] must be technically 
and economically feasible, [3] must be within the BLM’s authority to implement, and [4] must 
address unresolved conflicts about the proposed action with respect to alternative uses of 
available resources. In the existing 2023 Environmental Assessment, the justifications for why 
certain potential action alternatives were considered but eliminated from detailed analysis remain 
relevant and applicable to the new proposed action.  
A description of the potential action alternatives that were considered but eliminated from 
detailed analysis is presented in the existing BLM NEPA document (DOI-BLM-NV-S010-2023-
0016-EA) on page 12. 
In addition, it is important to note that the BLM ultimately approved (via its NEPA decision 
record) and authorized (via a ROW grant) the original 2023 proposed action. Consequently, this 
means that a potential denial of the new proposed action may result in the construction and 
operation of the original 2023 proposed action coming to fruition since it is already approved and 
authorized. In other words, a denial of the new proposed action does not mean that the project 
area in question would remain undeveloped.  
4. Is existing analysis valid considering any new information or circumstances relevant to 
the proposed action (such as, rangeland health standards assessment, recent 
endangered species listings, updated list of BLM sensitive species)?  Can you reasonably 
conclude that new information and new circumstances do not warrant substantial 
change to the analysis of the new proposed action? 
Yes. The analysis provided in the existing 2023 Environmental Assessment remains valid with 
regards to the new proposed action. Because the 2023 Environmental Assessment is still less 
than three years old and there are no known new circumstances or information regarding the 
condition of resources present at the project location, the 2023 Environmental Assessment 
remains relevant to the new proposed action with regards to alternatives, affected environment, 
effects analysis, and mitigation measures. The project area is still surrounded by intensive land 
uses and heavy infrastructure including open-pit aggregate quarries, utility-scale solar 
photovoltaic farms, electric transmission corridors, a highway corridor, and an outdoor firearms 
shooting range.

5. Are the environmental effects that would result from implementation of the new 
proposed action similar (both quantitatively and qualitatively) to those analyzed in the 
existing NEPA document? 
Yes. The effects analysis in the existing 2023 Environmental Assessment considered the original 
proposed action’s potential effects on 14 distinct resources. Below is a concise resource-by-
resource explanation as to why the existing analysis remains applicable to the new proposed 
action. 
o Air Quality (AQ) – Because the new proposed action’s location, size, and amount of ground 
disturbance remain the same as the original 2023 proposed action, and construction duration, 
construction equipment, and vehicle types and quantities are similar to the original 2023 
proposed action, construction phase impacts from dust and equipment emissions are likely to 
remain similar as well, as are local AQ regulatory/permitting requirements.   
Operational phase on-site impacts on AQ are similar, since both projects do not directly 
generate large volumes of pollutant emissions, but may generate some emissions during 
periodic use of emergency backup generators.   
The potential fugitive emissions associated with the data center’s air-cooling system are 
expected to be de minimis under Clean Air Act standards. Because the cooling compounds 
must be predominantly non-volatile / non-HFC (per 40 CFR Part 84) and the system is 
sealed, the Potential to Emit (PTE) falls below the threshold for Hazardous Air Pollutants 
(HAPs), thereby exempting the cooling infrastructure from major source air permitting 
requirements. 
Operational phase off-site pollutant emissions resulting from the generation of electricity for 
the new proposed action will be limited, since the new proposed action will receive its 
electrical power wholesale from the Western Area Power Administration’s (WAPA’s) Mead 
Substation. Mead Substation’s physical inputs are dominated by the 2,074 MW Hoover Dam 
hydroelectric complex plus gigawatts of localized solar generation within the Eldorado 
Valley Energy Zone (such as the Copper Mountain and Townsite Solar projects). 
Consequently, during peak daylight hours, most of the power pooled and exported through 
the substation is sourced from non-carbon emitting renewable generation. With an estimated 
peak demand of up to 167 MW, the new proposed action represents less than 2% of the Mead 
Substation’s total transport capacity. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.2 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Cultural Resources – Because the new proposed action’s location, size, and amount of 
ground disturbance remain the same as the original 2023 proposed action, the potential for 
physical impacts to cultural resources remains the same as the original 2023 proposed action. 
Because the indirect APE has already been substantially impacted by surrounding land uses / 
development, the new proposed action’s potential visual and atmospheric effects on cultural 
resources would be unchanged from the original 2023 proposed action.  
The environmental analysis in Section 3.3 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action.  
[[page 8]]
o Socioeconomics – Estimated workforce sizes for the new proposed action are slightly larger 
for both the construction phase and the operational phase in comparison to the original 2023 
proposed action, thereby potentially resulting in a nominally increased benefit to the local 
labor market. According to the Applicant, the new proposed action will create 250-300 jobs 
during construction, and 40 full-time positions during the operational life of the project.  
The environmental analysis in Section 3.4 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Fish and Wildlife (excluding T&E species) – Because the new proposed action’s location, 
size, and amount of ground disturbance remain the same as the original 2023 proposed 
action, the potential for adverse impacts to wildlife habitat remains the same as the original 
2023 proposed action.  Although some specific types of potential impacts within the project 
area itself could be replaced by other impacts (e.g., “lake effect” on avian species would be 
no longer present, while new noise effects may occur), these differences in effects could be 
minor considering the surrounding lands and resources are already heavily disturbed with 
hundreds of acres of solar photovoltaic panels as well as noise-inducing mining quarry 
operations, high voltage powerline corridors, a highway corridor, and a firearms shooting 
range. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.5 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Fuels and Fire Management – The new proposed action’s location remains the same as the 
original 2023 proposed action. The Existing 2023 Environmental assessment explains that 
the project area and immediate surrounding areas are open desert with minimal vegetation 
that do not support meaningful levels of vegetative fuel to carry wildland fire. Although a 
data center poses a larger fire risk than solar panel arrays, the project component with the 
greatest risk for fire – the battery energy storage system (BESS) – is common to both the 
original 2023 proposed action and the new proposed action. The existing 2023 
Environmental Assessment analyzes the potential fire risk of an on-site BESS and prescribes 
mitigation measures, including development of a fire management plan, that remain valid 
with regards to the new proposed action. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.6 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Meteorological Trends – The analysis presented in the existing 2023 Environmental 
Assessment states that emissions known to impact meteorological trends “would be 
generated under the [original 2023] Proposed Action through the use of vehicles and off-road 
equipment during Project construction and decommissioning, with minimal emissions 
generated during O&M.” This assessment holds true for the new proposed action, as the 
construction equipment and construction duration are largely similar. 
As noted above (in the Air Quality section), operational phase off-site emissions resulting 
from the electrical demand of the new proposed action will be limited, since it will receive its 
electrical power from WAPA’s Mead Substation which has an energy generation mix that is 
majority renewable generation during solar production hours. With an estimated peak 
demand of up to 167 MW, the new proposed action represents less than 2% of the Mead 
Substation’s total transport capacity. 
[[page 9]]
Although the new proposed action poses a slight risk of emitting chemical pollutants that can 
contribute to changing meteorological trends (due to potential fugitive emissions of 
chemicals used for refrigerant purposes), these emissions are unquestionably negligible in 
comparison to the major sources of such emissions (generally speaking, the transportation 
sector and the electrical power generation sector). In addition, the cooling compounds must 
be predominantly non-volatile / non-HFC (per 40 CFR Part 84) and the system is sealed, 
further reducing the chance for emissions of HFC chemicals.  
The 2023 EA states, “On a global scale, the GHG emission contribution of any single source 
on a subnational scale is dwarfed by the large number of comparable national and 
subnational contributors…. A single subnational contributor is very unlikely to influence 
global cumulative emissions. The [original 2023] Proposed Action alone is very unlikely to 
influence global cumulative emissions on a scale to make observable any increase or 
lessening of the social cost of all global emissions.” This assessment remains true for the new 
proposed action as well. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.7 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action.  
o Migratory Birds and Eagles – The existing 2023 Environmental Assessment states that 
“Impacts to migratory birds (including golden eagles) are quantified as either displacement or 
removal of habitat and mortality. Due to a lack of suitable nesting habitat, impacts to golden 
eagles are limited to the amount of foraging habitat that would be permanently removed by 
the [original 2023] Proposed Action.” Because the new proposed action will utilize the same 
location with the same acreage of disturbance as the original 2023 Proposed Action, this 
assessment holds true. The 2023 Environmental Assessment explains that impacts to nesting 
and foraging for golden eagles will be minimal, due to the small scale of the project and the 
availability of potential foraging habitat to the southeast and west of the project. 
Regarding migratory birds, the 2023 Environmental Assessment discusses that most impacts 
to these species would be caused by the loss of 85 acres of habitat (rather than the type of 
project proposed). Because the new proposed action will occur in the same location, this 
assessment remains accurate. The 2023 Environmental Assessment also explains that 
“Waterbird species may potentially mistake the solar panels for water features on which the 
birds can land…. These behaviors may lead to collisions with PV solar panels resulting in 
mortality, injury, or stranding of those species that require water to take off again”. This 
unfortunate phenomenon is known as the “lake effect hypothesis”. Switching the proposed 
action from a solar facility to a data center may avoid the potential for this particular adverse 
effect on waterbirds. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.8 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Recreation / Travel / Wild and Scenic Rivers – The existing 2023 EA demonstrates that 
potential impacts to these resources are mostly due to the original 2023 proposed action’s 
location, access routes, construction duration and methods, and perimeter fencing needs. 
Since these project characteristics remain very similar for the new proposed action, it is 
reasonable to conclude that the effects would remain similar as well, thereby resulting in 
analysis in the 2023 EA that is adequate to cover the new proposed action. 
[[page 10]]
The environmental analysis in Section 3.9 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Soils and Surface Hydrologic Conditions – The existing 2023 Environmental Assessment 
demonstrates that potential impacts to these resources are mostly due to the original 2023 
proposed action’s location, construction duration and methods, and overall surface grading. 
Since these project characteristics remain very similar for the new proposed action, it is 
reasonable to conclude that the effects would remain similar as well, thereby resulting in 
analysis in the 2023 Environmental Assessment that is adequate to cover the new proposed 
action. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.10 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Special Designations – Because the new proposed action is in the same location as the 
original 2023 proposed action, the proximity to specially designated lands – specifically the 
Old Spanish National Historic Trail – does not change. Because the intensity of development 
and general type of development does not change (modern industrial use with relatively low-
height improvements, in an area presently occupied with active mining operations, solar 
power generation facilities, and multiple high-voltage electrical transmission lines), the 
impacts to nearby specially designated lands is likely very similar to what is analyzed in the 
existing 2023 Environmental Assessment. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.11 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Threatened, Endangered, or Candidate Animal Species – Because the new proposed 
action is in the same location as the original 2023 proposed action, the list of T&E species 
which may have their habitat impacted does not change. Because the new proposed action’s 
location, construction duration and methods, and intensity of development remain the same, 
it is reasonable to conclude that impacts, mitigation measures, and the outcome of 
interagency consultation would remain the same as well, thereby resulting in analysis in the 
2023 EA that is adequate to cover the new proposed action. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.12 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Vegetation – Because the new proposed action is in the same location as the original 2023 
proposed action, the type of vegetative communities and the list of botanical species which 
may be present in the project area (including special-status species) does not change. Because 
the new proposed action’s location, construction duration and methods, and intensity of 
development (including grading) remain the same or similar, it is reasonable to conclude that 
impacts and mitigation measures would remain the same as well, thereby resulting in analysis 
in the 2023 EA that is adequate to cover the new proposed action. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.13 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Visual Resources – Although certain structures in the new proposed action would be 
different from the original 2023 proposed action, the general type of land use would remain 
the same (modern industrial use with relatively low-height structures and improvements) as 
would the location, intensity of use, size of the project area, and perimeter shape.  
[[page 11]]
As the 2023 Environmental Assessment explains, the project area is currently designated as 
Visual Resource Inventory (VRI) Class IV. The new proposed action is unlikely to trigger a 
change in this VRI designation. The surrounding land uses which contribute to the VRI class 
would remain the same (active mining operations, solar power generation facilities, and 
multiple high-voltage electrical transmission lines).  
The Visual Resource Management (VRM) designation for the project area is Class III. 
Because the new proposed action is similar to the original 2023 proposed action in size, 
location, and technologically-advanced industrial use, it is reasonable to conclude that the 
degree of visual impact felt from the new proposed action at the selected KOP’s would 
remain weak, and that the new proposed action would remain in conformance with BLM’s 
VRM objectives associated with the VRM Class III (2023 EA p. 58). 
No longer including photovoltaic solar panels as part of the project area’s development may 
improve the glare analysis for the new proposed action. VR mitigation measures specified in 
Appendix H of the exiting 2023 Environmental Assessment would remain applicable. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.14 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Wastes (Hazardous or Solid) – Because the new proposed action will implement 
geotechnical testing and utilize construction equipment and methods similar to the original 
2023 proposed action, the construction phase impacts will be similar as well. During the 
operational life of the new proposed action, limited quantities of hazardous materials will be 
used on site that are similar to the types of hazardous materials that were considered in the 
2023 Environmental Assessment. Thus, potential impacts would be similar. Mitigation 
measures prescribed in the 2023 Environmental Assessment (including development of and 
adherence to a hazmat management plan, spill prevention plan, and an emergency response 
plan) would remain appropriate for and applicable to the new proposed action. 
Any liquid waste resulting from periodic maintenance of the closed-loop cooling system will 
be trapped and properly disposed of offsite through contractors approved to handle and 
dispose of the specific mixture in use. 
The environmental analysis in Section 3.15 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Effects of Past, Present, and Reasonably Foreseeable Future Actions – The new proposed 
action is in the same location as the original 2023 proposed action with the same resource 
conditions and is similar in type and intensity of land use (modern high-technology 
electrically-focused industrial land use). Therefore, the anticipated impacts of the new 
proposed action when combined with the effects of past, present, and reasonably foreseeable 
future actions in the analysis area are sufficiently similar to the impacts that were analyzed 
and disclosed in the 2023 Environmental Assessment.  
The environmental analysis in Section 4 of the 2023 Environmental Assessment is 
appropriate with regard to the new proposed action. 
o Notes on water use: 
The 2023 Environmental Assessment for the proposed solar project analyzed the Applicant’s 
intended use of reclaimed effluent water discharged by the City of Boulder City Wastewater 
[[page 12]]
Treatment Plant for construction phase dust suppression. The Applicant’s 2026 POD for the 
new proposed action (data center) explains that this plan for sourcing dust suppression water 
remains unchanged. Although the volume of water estimated for dust suppression use is 
greater for the new proposed action (now projected at up to 500,000 gallons per day), this 
consumptive use will not adversely affect regional water supply volume. This is because – 
unlike other communities in southern Nevada – reclaimed effluent water discharged by the 
City of Boulder City Wastewater Treatment Plant is not recycled and returned to the 
Colorado River / Lake Mead system. Rather, Boulder City’s reclaimed effluent water is sent 
to local evaporation ponds where this water is ultimately lost to the atmosphere. The 2026 
POD further states that the Applicant does not intend to develop or purchase water rights 
(2026 POD p. 21).    
During the operational life of the project, daily water usage estimates for the new proposed 
action are in the +/- 2,000 gallon-per-day range and are primarily for uses such as employee 
restroom and kitchen facilities (2026 POD p. 40). This estimate converts to approximately 
2.24 acre-feet per year, which is consistent with the water usage estimate in the existing 2023 
Environmental Assessment of 2-3 acre-feet per year (2023 EA p. 10). (Under the new 
proposed action, employee numbers would increase, but washing of solar panels would be 
eliminated.) 
According to the Applicant’s Plan of Development, approximately 90% of the potable water 
used onsite during the operational life of the project will be returned to Boulder City’s 
wastewater treatment plant. 
The Applicant proposes a design for the new proposed action that would rely on a closed-
loop air-cooling system for the data center. Up to 400,000 gallons (1.23 acre-feet) of a 
purified water/propylene glycol mix would be required for an initial one-time filling of the 
closed loop system, but this type of cooling system does not rely upon constant evaporation 
(and an associated loss of water) as its means of heat dissipation. The purified 
water/propylene glycol mix will be purchased from a vendor of that mixture. The cooling 
system will not be filled with any municipal water, as it is not appropriate for this type of 
cooling system. The initial fill of the cooling system and any topping up of the system must 
be with the purchased purified water/propylene glycol mix. The first year, one-time 
consumption of 1.23 acre-feet would be 55% greater than the Applicant’s estimated per year 
average water use of 2.24 acre-feet, but when considered across the proposed 30-year life of 
the project, the project remains well within the existing 2023 Environmental Assessment’s 
water usage estimate of 2 to 3 acre-feet per year. 
It is estimated that up to an additional 2,000 gallons (0.006 acre-feet) per year of purified 
water/propylene glycol mix could be needed for periodic maintenance activities of the 
cooling system and will be likewise sourced from a vendor of that mixture. Any waste from 
the system associated with periodic maintenance will be trapped and properly disposed of 
offsite through contractors approved to handle and dispose of the material.

I. DECISION
I have decided to approve the application from  Townsite Solar 2 LLC (Applicant) , wherein the 
Applicant seeks an amendment to their existing BLM ROW grant NVN-99875. The original grant 
authorizes the Applicant to construct, operate, maintain, and decommission a 19 MW solar project 
plus a 35 MW Battery Storage System (BESS). Now, the Applicant is requesting authorization in 
their new proposal to construct, own, operate, maintain, and eventually decommission a data center 
on 81 acres of the original grant’s 85-acre ROW area, with an additional 4 acres to be used for an 
80 × 2,000–foot linear facilities segment (Project / new proposed action).  The original proposal 
for the solar project and BESS was not constructed. The new proposed action will be located within 
the same footprint as the original grant. 
Details regarding the design, components, and construction methods  of the new proposed data 
center are provided in the Applicant’s plan of development (POD – dated May 2026).  
Details regarding the BLM’s evaluation of the existing NEPA document’s adequacy in analyzing 
the new proposed action are provided in DNA documentation DOI -BLM-NV-S010-2026-0080-
DNA. That evaluation forms the basis for my rationale in approving the Applicant’s request  
(Section III).

The new proposed action is in conformance with the 1998 Las Vegas RMP. Specifically, it

Meet public demand and reduce impacts to sensitive resources by providing an orderly
