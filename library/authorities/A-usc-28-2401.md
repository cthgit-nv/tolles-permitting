## What it is

The general statute of limitations for non-tort civil actions against the United States. It is the default limitations period for Administrative Procedure Act challenges to agency decisions, including NEPA, NHPA and FLPMA claims against BLM or Reclamation decisions, where no specific statute sets a shorter one. It was enacted June 25, 1948, ch. 646, 62 Stat. 971.

## Operative text or holding

A civil action against the United States is barred unless the complaint is filed within six years after the right of action first accrues, except as provided for contract disputes under chapter 71 of title 41. Subsection (b) covers tort claims and does not apply to APA review.

## Quotes

> "every civil action commenced against the United States shall be barred unless the complaint is filed within six years after the right of action first accrues." (28 U.S.C. 2401(a))
> "A tort claim against the United States shall be forever barred unless it is presented in writing to the appropriate Federal agency within two years after such claim accrues" (28 U.S.C. 2401(b))

## Notes

- Accrual: the Supreme Court's 2024 decision in Corner Post, Inc. v. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System addressed when an APA claim "first accrues" under 2401(a). The prevailing summary is that accrual occurs when the plaintiff is injured by final agency action, not when the action issues. The opinion was not retrieved in this session and has no card. Do not cite it without a case card.
- Application to our projects: without FAST-41 covered status (see A-usc-42-4370m, which sets 2 years from Federal Register notice), a BLM ROW grant or Reclamation Part 429 authorization for Hazen or Jean is open to APA challenge for six years. Under an injury-based accrual rule, the window could run longer for a later-injured plaintiff.
- Statute-specific periods can be shorter. None was identified for FLPMA ROW grants or Part 429 in this session.
