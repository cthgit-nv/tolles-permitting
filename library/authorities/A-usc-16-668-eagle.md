## What it is

The Eagle Act prohibits take of bald or golden eagles "without being permitted to do so" under the Act. It authorizes the Secretary to permit take by regulation. Unlike the MBTA, it has an operating incidental take permit program. The 2024 rule (50 CFR part 22, subpart E) includes a general permit specifically for power lines. `decided` is the 2024 rule's publication date.

## Operative text or holding

- Criminal and civil penalties (668(a)-(b)). Civil liability does not require knowing conduct.
- 668a authorizes permits by regulation, including taking golden eagle nests that interfere with resource development.
- 50 CFR 22.260 authorizes incidental take associated with power line activities. An applicant qualifies for a general permit by meeting 22.210. The general permit issues automatically on registration, with a maximum tenure of 5 years. Its conditions include:
  - reactive and proactive retrofit strategies;
  - collision and shooting response strategies;
  - avian-safe new construction in high eagle-risk areas;
  - siting away from eagle-use areas such as nests and winter roosts.
- A specific permit is available where a project is not eligible for the general permit.

## Quotes

> "without being permitted to do so as provided in this subchapter, shall take, possess, sell, purchase, barter, offer to sell, purchase or barter, transport, export or import, at any time or in any manner, any bald eagle, commonly known as the American eagle, or any golden eagle" (16 U.S.C. 668(b))
> "The regulations in this section authorize the incidental killing or injury of bald eagles and golden eagles associated with power line activities." (50 CFR 22.260(a))
> "Ensure that all new construction and rebuild or replacement of poles in areas of high risk for eagles is avian-safe" (50 CFR 22.260(d)(6))
> "General permits have a maximum tenure of 5 years." (50 CFR 22.210(f))

## Notes

- A permit is voluntary, but it is the only protection against Eagle Act liability for incidental take. Golden eagles are present across Nevada's Great Basin and Mojave. Nest locations near either site were not researched.
- Hazen and Jean: the transmission lines and substations are "power line activities". The general permit is designed around utility service areas and existing infrastructure baselines. Whether a new project developer who is not a utility, such as Jean Data Partners LLC, fits the general permit, or should seek a specific permit, was not resolved. Read 22.260 and 22.210 in full with the applicant's facts.
- Registering for a general permit requires certifying that the activity complies with other federal, state, tribal and local laws, including a certification concerning properties listed or eligible for the National Register (22.210(c); see source file). NHPA Section 106 work for the ROW should therefore be finished or tracked before registration.
