## What it is

Supreme Court decision (Alito, J., for five Justices; Thomas, J., concurring, joined by Gorsuch, J.; Kagan, J., and Kavanaugh, J., each concurring in the judgment, joined by others) on the reach of the Clean Water Act to wetlands. No. 21-454.

## Operative text or holding

"Waters of the United States" covers only relatively permanent, standing or continuously flowing bodies of water forming geographic features (streams, oceans, rivers, lakes), adopting the Rapanos plurality. Adjacent wetlands are covered only if they have a continuous surface connection to such a water, so that they are indistinguishable from it. The Court says the CWA never mentions the significant nexus test, so EPA has no statutory basis to impose it (slip op. at 24). For our NEPA work it matters because Corps section 404 jurisdiction is often the only federal hook, and ephemeral washes may fall outside it.

## Quotes

> "we conclude that the Rapanos plurality was correct: the CWA’s use of “waters” encompasses “only those relatively permanent, standing or continuously flowing bodies of water ‘forming geographic[al] features’ that are described in ordinary parlance as ‘streams, oceans, rivers, and lakes.’”" (598 U.S. at 671; slip op. at 14)
> "In sum, we hold that the CWA extends to only those wetlands that are “as a practical matter indistinguishable from waters of the United States.”" (598 U.S. at 678; slip op. at 22)
> "This holding compels reversal here. The wetlands on the Sacketts’ property are distinguishable from any possibly covered waters." (598 U.S. at 684; slip op. at 27)
