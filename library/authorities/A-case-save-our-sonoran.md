## What it is

Ninth Circuit amended opinion affirming a preliminary injunction against a 608-acre residential development near Phoenix, where the Corps had limited its NEPA review for section 404 permits to the desert washes being filled.

## Operative text or holding

Because the jurisdictional washes ran throughout the property "like lines run through graph paper," any Corps-permitted development would affect the whole property, so the Corps had to analyze the entire development under NEPA, and the district court could enjoin the whole project. The applicant could not narrow the Corps' NEPA review by submitting 66 separate permit sites. Wetlands Action Network was distinguished because there the upland development was separable from the wetlands fill.

## Quotes

> "The district court correctly held that the Corps had improperly constrained its NEPA analysis to the washes, rather than considering the development’s effect on the environment as a whole." (408 F.3d at 1121)
> "The Corps’ responsibility under NEPA to consider the environmental consequences of a permit extends even to environmental effects with no impact on jurisdictional waters at all." (408 F.3d at 1122)
> "However, the scope of the Corps’ responsibility under NEPA is not dictated by the applicant; rather, it is directed by statute." (408 F.3d at 1122)
> "The NEPA analysis should have included the entire property." (408 F.3d at 1122)

## Treatment

2026-09-24: CourtListener shows the case cited by 103 opinions. No express negative treatment found; CourtListener full-text search for "Save Our Sonoran" with "Seven County" after June 1, 2025 returned no results.
2026-09-24: status set to questioned on our own reading, not on a court's holding. (1) Seven County directs substantial deference to the agency's line drawing on scope (605 U.S. at 182, 192) and says an agency need not assess separate projects outside its authority (605 U.S. at 188, 190). Save Our Sonoran reviewed the Corps' scope choice less deferentially and states a broad NEPA responsibility reaching effects with no impact on jurisdictional waters (408 F.3d at 1122). (2) Its whole-project result rested on the desert washes being jurisdictional waters under the "other waters" provision, 33 C.F.R. 328.3(a)(3) as then written (408 F.3d at 1123). Sackett (598 U.S. at 671, 678) limits "waters" to relatively permanent bodies of water and adjacent wetlands with a continuous surface connection, and the current rule excludes swales and erosional features including small washes (see A-cfr-33-328-3). Ephemeral desert washes like those in Save Our Sonoran may no longer be jurisdictional, which would remove the premise.

## Notes

- Dates: original opinion filed April 26, 2004; amended opinion filed May 25, 2005 on denial of rehearing (source file header). The 408 F.3d 1113 cite is to the amended opinion; `decided` above uses the amendment date. CourtListener lists the date filed as May 25, 2005.
- The opinion cites Sylvester as 884 F.2d 394, 398 to 99 (the Sylvester I opinion), not 882 F.2d 407. See A-case-sylvester Notes.
- This is the leading Ninth Circuit authority for the adverse position that a Corps permit can pull an entire private development into NEPA scope. The main answers are the Sackett jurisdiction point and Seven County deference, plus Wetlands Action Network (A-case-wetlands-action) on separability.
- Preliminary injunction posture: the holding is that the district court did not abuse its discretion in finding serious questions on the merits.
