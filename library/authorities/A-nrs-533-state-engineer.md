## What it is

The core provisions of Nevada water law. Water belongs to the public. A right is acquired only by appropriation for beneficial use. Anyone who wants to appropriate water, or to change the point of diversion, manner of use or place of use of an existing right, must first get a permit from the State Engineer (Division of Water Resources). `decided` uses the 1913 water code date, which is not verified. The session laws cite "[1:140:1913]".

## Operative text or holding

- All water, surface and underground, belongs to the public (533.025). It may be appropriated only for beneficial use as provided in the chapter (533.030(1)). Beneficial use is the basis, measure and limit of the right (533.035).
- A permit application to the State Engineer is required before any work on a new appropriation or on a change of an existing right (533.325).
- The State Engineer must approve a proper application if the fees are paid and the applicant shows good faith intent and financial ability to put the water to beneficial use with reasonable diligence (533.370(1)). The State Engineer must reject an application where there is no unappropriated water, the use conflicts with existing rights or protectable domestic well interests, or the use "threatens to prove detrimental to the public interest" (533.370(2)).

## Quotes

> "The water of all sources of water supply within the boundaries of the State whether above or beneath the surface of the ground, belongs to the public." (NRS 533.025)
> "Beneficial use shall be the basis, the measure and the limit of the right to the use of water." (NRS 533.035)
> "shall, before performing any work in connection with such appropriation, change in place of diversion or change in manner or place of use, apply to the State Engineer for a permit to do so." (NRS 533.325)

## Notes

- This card is deliberately short. The groundwater provisions (NRS Chapter 534, basin designation, well permits) were not read. For Hazen, groundwater appropriation or change applications in the relevant hydrographic basin will fall mostly under Chapter 534 as well as 533. Truckee and Carson River decreed rights (the Newlands Project and the Orr Ditch and Alpine decrees) add a federal decree court layer. Neither was researched here.
- UEPA interaction: NRS 704.848(2) excludes the State Engineer from "other permitting entity". The UEPA 120- and 150-day decision clocks and the rule against duplicative review therefore do not bind the State Engineer. Water for the gas plant, geothermal plant or data center cooling runs on the State Engineer's own timetable (533.370(4) sets a general 2-year decision period after the protest deadline, with exceptions).
- Water facilities that support a generating plant are "associated facilities" under NRS 704.860(1), so they also fall within UEPA. See A-nrs-704-uepa.
