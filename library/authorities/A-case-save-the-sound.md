## What it is

D.C. Circuit decision (Katsas, J., for a panel of Srinivasan, C.J., Wilkins and Katsas, JJ.) denying petitions for review of the FAA's EA and FONSI for a runway extension and new terminal at Tweed New Haven Airport, Connecticut. Consolidated with No. 24-1029 (Town of East Haven).

## Operative text or holding

Applying Seven County, the court rejected a segmentation claim that the FAA should have included possible future taxiway upgrades. NEPA never requires an agency to evaluate a separate project, and courts defer to a reasonable and manageable line. Under the CEQ regulation in effect at the time (40 C.F.R. 1501.9(e) (2023)) and City of Boston Delegation v. FERC, the court asked whether the projects overlap in time and have substantial independent utility. The FAA drew a reasonable line: the taxiway project was temporally remote (no final approval sought within five years), each project had substantial independent value, and neither the runway nor the terminal required the taxiway upgrades. The court also rejected cumulative effects, enplanement forecast, and wetlands and stormwater claims.

## Quotes

> "we police this line by considering whether possibly different projects overlap temporally and have substantial independent utility." (slip op. at 8)
> "For one thing, the FAA explained, the taxiway project is temporally remote." (slip op. at 9)
> "Moreover, the respective projects have substantial independent value." (slip op. at 9)
> "So, neither the runway expansion nor the terminal improvements required the taxiway upgrades to go forward." (slip op. at 10)

## Notes

- Counsel cites this case as 2026 U.S. App. LEXIS 21635. That LEXIS number could not be verified from free sources. The only parallel cite seen is 2026 WL 2093931 (in For a Better Bayou). Use the slip opinion cite with docket number until a reporter cite issues.
- Counsel's summary of the factors (temporal separation, substantial independent value, whether the activities require one another) matches the opinion (slip op. at 8 to 10). Note that the court grounded the temporal overlap and independent utility test in the 2023 CEQ regulation and City of Boston Delegation, 897 F.3d 241, 252, and said in footnote 1 that it applied the CEQ regulations in effect when the FAA decided, without resolving the effect of Marin Audubon or the CEQ rescission (91 Fed. Reg. 618 (Jan. 8, 2026), as cited in the opinion). The "require one another" factor is the court's response to the petitioners' safety-standards argument rather than a separately named factor.
- The opinion quotes Seven County at 605 U.S. at 182, 187, 189, 190. It also describes the FAA's practice of conditional approval before NEPA review ("not yet needed and … not ripe for decision," FAA Order 5050.4B), which may be useful on phased approvals.
- On the five-year line: the court distinguished Northern Plains Resource Council v. STB, 668 F.3d 1067 (9th Cir. 2011), and said Seven County prevents requiring analysis of a distinct, inchoate project expected to begin more than five years after the approval (slip op. at 11).
