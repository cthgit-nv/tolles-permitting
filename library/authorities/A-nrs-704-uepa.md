## What it is

Nevada's Utility Environmental Protection Act (UEPA). No person other than a local government may begin construction of a "utility facility" without a permit from the Public Utilities Commission of Nevada (PUCN). The LCB page is stamped "[Rev. 4/15/2026 4:18:40 PM--2025]", so it reflects the 2025 session. `decided` is the 1971 enactment year (1971 Stat. 554), not a verified effective date.

## Operative text or holding

- Permit requirement (704.865(1)): no person other than a local government may "commence to construct" a utility facility without a PUCN permit. Replacing an existing facility with a like facility is not construction. "Commence to construct" (704.840) means clearing, excavation or other action that would adversely affect the natural environment of the site or route. Geotechnical borings are excluded.
- "Utility facility" (704.860):
  - (1) electric generating plants and their associated facilities. The exception covers plants whose primary energy source is renewable energy (as defined in NRS 704.7715) and whose nameplate capacity is 70 MW or less. "Associated facilities" includes water supply and wastewater facilities that serve the plant.
  - (2) electric transmission lines and transmission substations that meet all three tests: designed to operate at 200 kV or more, not required by local ordinance to be placed underground, and built outside any incorporated city.
  - (3) gas transmission lines, storage plants, compressor stations and their associated facilities built outside any incorporated city.
  - (4) water storage, transmission and treatment facilities, other than mining water.
  - (5) sewer transmission and treatment facilities.
  - The definition sets no length threshold for lines.
- Exclusions (704.865(3)):
  - (a) and (b) are pre-July 1, 1971 grandfather clauses, which can be waived under 704.865(4).
  - (c) a facility "over which an agency of the Federal Government has exclusive jurisdiction".
  - (d) certain facilities owned by suppliers under NRS 704.673 or 704.675 that are subject to NEPA and not jointly owned.
- Federal coordination:
  - 704.863(2): UEPA exempts nothing from federal requirements.
  - 704.870(2): if a federal agency must do an environmental analysis, the applicant must file a notice with the PUCN and each "other permitting entity" no later than the day it files the federal application. It must then file the full UEPA application within 30 days after the final EA or final EIS issues. The trigger is the final EA or EIS, not the ROD.
  - 704.877: the PUCN and other permitting entities must accept and incorporate the findings of an environmental review already done by an appropriate federal agency, and may not do a duplicative review.
  - 704.8905(1)(b): the PUCN must grant or deny within 120 days of an application filed on the federal track, and within 150 days on the non-federal track (704.8905(1)(a)).
  - 704.900: the PUCN may hold joint hearings and issue joint orders with federal agencies.
- Findings (704.890(1)):
  - probable environmental effect;
  - for a facility that emits greenhouse gases and is not renewable-primary, the extent to which it is needed for reliable service to customers in the State;
  - that need balances adverse effect;
  - minimum adverse effect;
  - conformity with state and local law, and that other permits are obtained or in process;
  - an adequate surplus asset retirement plan;
  - the public interest.
  The permit is conditioned on obtaining the other permits (704.890(2)).
- 704.873 gives the PUCN exclusive jurisdiction over need, but only where the applicant is a public utility subject to the resource planning statutes (NRS 704.736 to 704.754).

## Quotes

> "Electric transmission lines and transmission substations that: (a) Are designed to operate at 200 kilovolts or more;" (NRS 704.860(2)(a))
> "A person, other than a local government, shall not commence to construct a utility facility in the State without first having obtained a permit therefor from the Commission." (NRS 704.865(1))
> "Not later than 30 days after the issuance by the appropriate federal agency of either the final environmental assessment or final environmental impact statement, but not the record of decision or similar document, relating to the construction of the utility facility:" (NRS 704.870(2)(b))
> "Shall not conduct any duplicative environmental review on the application." (NRS 704.877(1)(b))

## Notes

- Hazen (Application 2, FMDP):
  - Any transmission line or substation of 200 kV or more outside an incorporated city is a UEPA utility facility, whatever its length.
  - The gas plant is an electric generating plant with no size floor, because it is not renewable. It needs a UEPA permit and the greenhouse gas need finding in 704.890(1)(b).
  - Solar is exempt only if its nameplate is 70 MW or less. Treat battery storage paired with solar as an associated facility; that reading is not confirmed.
  - Geothermal of 70 MW or less is exempt.
  - Water and wastewater facilities that support a generating plant are part of the facility.
  - Because BLM prepares a NEPA document, the 704.870(2) notice must be filed with the PUCN and the other permitting entities no later than the date the SF-299 (or the Reclamation application) is filed. This early-notice step is easy to miss.
- Unresolved:
  - (1) Does a behind-the-meter gas plant that serves only the data center fall outside UEPA? NRS 704.855(2) excludes self-consumed generation from "public utility", but 704.860 defines "utility facility" without reference to "public utility", and 704.865 applies to any "person". Counsel should answer this.
  - (2) Whether the Great Basin Gas Transmission pipeline is FERC-certificated, so that the 704.865(3)(c) federal exclusive-jurisdiction exclusion applies, was not verified.
- Jean:
  - Jean is unincorporated. A substation or line at 200 kV or more would be a utility facility. Voltage was not confirmed.
  - Fiber and a low-voltage access road are not within 704.860.
- "Other permitting entity" (704.848) excludes the State Engineer. See A-nrs-533-state-engineer.
