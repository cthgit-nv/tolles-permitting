## What it is

Supreme Court decision (Thomas, J., for the Court; the source text shows no separate opinions) holding that the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration did not have to analyze the environmental effects of Mexican trucks entering the United States when it issued safety regulations, because the President, not FMCSA, controlled whether the trucks could enter.

## Operative text or holding

But-for causation is not enough to make an agency responsible under NEPA for an effect. NEPA requires a reasonably close causal relationship, like proximate cause, and the rule of reason. Where an agency has no ability to prevent an effect because of its limited statutory authority, it is not a legally relevant cause of that effect and need not consider it.

## Quotes

> "However, a “but for” causal relationship is insufficient to make an agency responsible for a particular effect under NEPA and the relevant regulations." (541 U.S. at 767)
> "It would not, therefore, satisfy NEPA’s “rule of reason” to require an agency to prepare a full EIS due to the environmental impact of an action it could not refuse to perform." (541 U.S. at 769)
> "We hold that where an agency has no ability to prevent a certain effect due to its limited statutory authority over the relevant actions, the agency cannot be considered a legally relevant “cause” of the effect." (541 U.S. at 770)
