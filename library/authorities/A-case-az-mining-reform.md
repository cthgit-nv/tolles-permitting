## What it is

Ninth Circuit amended opinion (M. Smith, J., joined by Bress, J.; Rawlinson, J., dissenting in part on the appraisal issue) affirming denial of a preliminary injunction against the Oak Flat land exchange to Resolution Copper under the Southeast Arizona Land Exchange and Conservation Act. Consolidated appeals include San Carlos Apache Tribe v. U.S. Forest Service.

## Operative text or holding

Plaintiffs were unlikely to succeed on NEPA claims against the FEIS. On cumulative water impacts, Seven County means the Forest Service need not analyze a separate future development (the Superstition Vistas development, which plaintiffs described as a 275-square-mile "mega development"), especially where plaintiffs showed no Forest Service regulatory authority over it, and the agency's line between the portion with a demonstrated water source and the rest was a discretionary choice owed deference.

## Quotes

> "Under the circumscribed NEPA review standard the Supreme Court set out in Seven County, however, the Government’s water analysis as to the Superstition Vistas development suffices." (slip op. at 26)
> "This is especially true because AMRC has made no argument nor provided any evidence suggesting that the Forest Service has regulatory authority over the Superstition Vistas development." (slip op. at 26)

## Notes

- Preliminary injunction posture: holdings are about likelihood of success, not final merits.
- This is a separate-project (cumulative effects) case, not a segmentation case in the strict sense. Useful for the no-authority point in the Ninth Circuit.
- Cites Seven County at 605 U.S. at 180, 182, 183, 187, 188, 190, matching the U.S. pages confirmed on A-case-seven-county.
