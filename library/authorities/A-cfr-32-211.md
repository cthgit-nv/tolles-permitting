## What it is

DoD's procedures for reviewing energy and other projects for effects on military operations and readiness. There are two tracks:

- a formal review of projects filed with the FAA under 49 U.S.C. 44718 (FAA Form 7460-1 filings); and
- an informal review that a developer, landowner, or State, tribal or local official can request before filing.

The part implements section 358 of the FY2011 NDAA. Its source note is 78 FR 73088 (Dec. 5, 2013); `decided` uses that publication date.

## Operative text or holding

- Formal review (211.6): the Clearinghouse gets the application from DOT, and DoD Components comment within 20 days. Within 30 days the Clearinghouse finds one of three things: no adverse impact; an adverse impact too attenuated to need mitigation; or a possible adverse impact.
  - On a possible adverse impact, it offers mitigation discussions. The applicant must agree within 5 days, and discussions run up to 90 days unless extended in writing.
  - Only the senior officer (Deputy Secretary of Defense) may tell DOT that a project poses an "unacceptable risk to the national security".
- Informal review (211.7): the requester supplies location (latitude and longitude), height and nature of the project. Components comment within 30 days, and the Clearinghouse acts within 50 days of the request. An informal determination does not bind DoD or DOT.
- Unacceptable risk (211.3): endangering safety in air commerce related to DoD activities, interfering with navigable airspace use related to DoD, or significantly impairing DoD's ability to conduct training, testing and operations or to maintain readiness.

## Treatment

2026-09-24: eCFR text current to 2026-09-22. The authority line still cites Public Law 111-383, section 358, as amended by Public Law 112-81, section 331. Section 358 was later codified and restructured at 10 U.S.C. 183a (Pub. L. 115-91, 2017; last amended by Pub. L. 119-60, Dec. 18, 2025; see A-usc-10-183a). The statute now provides for a 75-day preliminary review, compared with the part's 30-day step. The part appears not to have been conformed. Status is set to questioned because the part's timelines may not match current statute. Where they differ, the statute controls.

## Notes

- Hazen: this is the most important non-NEPA review for the Hazen site because of NAS Fallon and the Fallon Range Training Complex, even though the Clearinghouse's formal jurisdiction runs through FAA filings.
  - Tall structures that could be affected include gas plant stacks, geothermal and cooling structures, transmission towers and data center buildings.
  - Solar glare and electromagnetic or radar effects near training airspace can also draw DoD objections.
  - Requesting an informal review early is voluntary but prudent.
- Jean: no military installation was identified in this session. Nellis and the Nevada Test and Training Range airspace were not mapped against Jean.
- The current Clearinghouse name in statute is the Military Aviation and Installation Assurance Clearinghouse (per A-usc-10-183a).
