## What it is

The Board affirmed a BLM Phoenix Field Office Decision Record and FONSI, based on an EA, granting a FLPMA Title V right-of-way for the Gila Bend/Ajo 230 kV transmission line, sited mostly in a designated 1-mile-wide corridor. The line would serve the reopening of a private copper mine. The Board denied a motion to dismiss and vacated a stay it had granted.

## Operative text or holding

- BLM may not segment one project into separate small actions to avoid finding significance, and an EA covers connected actions.
- Connected actions generally means Federal actions, but indirect effects include reasonably foreseeable effects of private actions that the Federal action causes. The test is functional interdependence and a reasonably close causal relationship.
- The mine's effects did not have to be analyzed as effects of the line, because the record showed the mine would reopen with or without the line (for example by refurbishing on-site generation). The line did not cause the mine, and the two were not connected.
- The EA's range of alternatives, including no action, was adequate.

## Quotes

> "Thus, BLM is precluded from segmenting a project into separate actions which might have an insignificant impact individually but a significant environmental impact collectively." (152 IBLA at 6)
> "will be denied when it appears from the record that the mining operation would proceed in the absence of approval of the right-of-way." (152 IBLA at 2, headnote 2)
> "We find no evidence that construction and operation of the Gila Bend/Ajo 230 kV transmission line itself will itself cause the reopening of the Ajo Copper Mine." (152 IBLA at 7)

## Notes

- Pins: reporter pages come from the OHA PDF footers.
- This decision predates Seven County and relies on the CEQ indirect effects definition and Ninth Circuit cases (Port of Astoria v. Hodel, Sierra Club v. Hodel, City of Davis v. Coleman, Sylvester). It is persuasive only.
- Relevance: it is the closest IBLA analog for a transmission line ROW serving private industrial load. It cuts both ways. If the private load (for example a data center at Jean) could not proceed without the BLM line, the Port of Astoria line of cases the Board distinguished would point toward analyzing that load's effects.
