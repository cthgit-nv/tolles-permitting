## What it is

Title 41 of the FAST Act (FAST-41), codified at 42 U.S.C. 4370m et seq. It creates the Federal Permitting Improvement Steering Council, the Permitting Dashboard and coordinated schedules for "covered projects". It also shortens the statute of limitations for challenges to covered project authorizations. Enacted by Pub. L. 114-94, Dec. 4, 2015; amended through Pub. L. 118-5 (2023).

## Operative text or holding

- Covered project (4370m(6)(A)): an activity in the United States that requires federal authorization or environmental review and involves construction of infrastructure in a listed sector. The listed sectors include renewable or conventional energy production, electricity transmission, pipelines, data storage and data management, high-performance computing, artificial intelligence, and energy storage. The project must also meet one of these tests:
  - (i) subject to NEPA, likely to require total investment over $200,000,000, and not eligible for abbreviated authorization or review;
  - (ii) covered by a CO2 pipeline programmatic review;
  - (iii) sponsored by a tribe on its land; or
  - (iv) subject to NEPA and, in the Council's opinion, of a size and complexity likely to benefit from enhanced oversight, such as requiring authorization from more than two federal agencies or an EIS.
- Authorization (4370m(3)) includes "any interagency consultation", so ESA section 7 and similar consultations are covered.
- Limitations (4370m-6(a)(1)): a claim seeking judicial review of an authorization for a covered project is barred unless filed within 2 years after the Federal Register notice of final agency action, unless a shorter period applies. For NEPA claims, the plaintiff must have commented during the environmental review with enough detail to put the lead agency on notice, unless no reasonable opportunity to comment was given.
- 4370m-6(b): courts considering preliminary injunctions must weigh effects on public health, safety, the environment and jobs, and may not presume those harms reparable.

## Quotes

> "is likely to require a total investment of more than $200,000,000; and" (42 U.S.C. 4370m(6)(A)(i)(II))
> "data storage and data management, cybersecurity, carbon capture, energy storage, or any other sector as determined by a majority vote of the Council" (42 U.S.C. 4370m(6)(A))
> "the claim is filed not later than 2 years after the date of publication in the Federal Register of notice of final agency action on the authorization, unless a shorter time is specified in the Federal law under which judicial review is allowed; and" (42 U.S.C. 4370m-6(a)(1)(A))
> "the claim is filed by a party that submitted a comment during the environmental review; and" (42 U.S.C. 4370m-6(a)(1)(B)(i))

## Notes

- Both projects plausibly meet 4370m(6)(A)(i) on the energy, transmission, storage and data center sectors if total investment exceeds $200 million. Covered status is not automatic: the project sponsor must submit a notice to the facilitating agency under 4370m-2(a), which was not read in this session. The Council or agency then decides whether to add the project.
- Precedent in the same district: BLM's July 2026 Mosey Solar NOI states that Mosey "was determined to be a covered project under Title 41" on January 17, 2025 (see A-guid-rmp-southern-nv).
- Litigation value: covered status cuts the default 6-year window under 28 U.S.C. 2401(a) (see A-usc-28-2401) to 2 years from the Federal Register notice of final agency action. This works only if the agency publishes that notice. BLM ROW decisions are not always noticed in the Federal Register, so the sponsor should ask for publication.
- NEPA itself (42 U.S.C. 4336a(g)(3), see A-usc-42-4336a) gives a project sponsor a court petition for missed EA or EIS deadlines. It does not set a limitations period for challenges. The general default remains 28 U.S.C. 2401(a) unless FAST-41 or another statute applies.
