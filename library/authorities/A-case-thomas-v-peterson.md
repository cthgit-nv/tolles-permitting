## What it is

Ninth Circuit decision (Sneed, J.) holding that the Forest Service had to prepare an EIS analyzing together a logging road in the Jersey Jack area of the Nezperce National Forest and the timber sales the road was built to serve. It also held the Forest Service violated ESA procedures for the gray wolf.

## Operative text or holding

The road and the timber sales were "connected actions" under the CEQ regulation then in force (40 C.F.R. 1508.25(a)(1) (1984)) because the sales could not proceed without the road and the road would not be built but for the sales. Under the highway segmentation cases, a segment may be reviewed alone only if it has independent utility, meaning the agency might reasonably build it alone. If the future sales are certain enough to justify building the road, they are certain enough to analyze with it.

## Quotes

> "Not to require this would permit dividing a project into multiple “actions,” each of which individually has an insignificant environmental impact, but which collectively have a substantial impact." (753 F.2d at 758)
> "It is clear that the timber sales cannot proceed without the road, and the road would not be built but for the contemplated timber sales." (753 F.2d at 758)
> "We conclude, therefore, that the road construction and the contemplated timber sales are inextricably intertwined, and that they are “connected actions” within the meaning of the CEQ regulations." (753 F.2d at 759)
> "Where agency actions are sufficiently related so as to be “connected” within the meaning of the CEQ regulations, the agency may not escape compliance with the regulations by proceeding with one action while characterizing the others as remote or speculative." (753 F.2d at 760)

2026-09-24: Seven County does not directly undercut the connected-actions holding. Both the road and the timber sales were Forest Service actions within its own authority, so Seven County's no-authority rationale (605 U.S. at 188) does not reach this fact pattern. Two things weaken it at the margin: Seven County's instruction to defer to the agency's line drawing (605 U.S. at 182, 190), and the rescission of the CEQ regulations, including the connected-actions definition, which Thomas applied (rescission noted in Sierra Club v. FERC (Cumberland), slip op. at 13 n.7, citing 90 Fed. Reg. 10610 (Feb. 25, 2025)). CourtListener full-text search for "Thomas v. Peterson" with "Seven County" after June 1, 2025 returned no results.
