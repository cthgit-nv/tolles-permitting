## What it is

District court decision (Gonzalez, J.) on cross-motions for summary judgment in a challenge to DOE Presidential Permits and BLM rights-of-way for two transmission lines connecting new power plants in or near Mexicali, Mexico to the Imperial Valley substation in California. No. 02-CV-513-IEG.

## Operative text or holding

The Mexican power plants were not part of the proposed federal action because they were outside U.S. jurisdiction and not subject to DOE control. But emissions from plant turbines for which the permitted line was the only export path (a but-for cause) were "effects" of the permits that NEPA required DOE to analyze, applying the Sylvester "links of a single chain" test. Turbines serving the Mexican market, or able to export through another line, were not effects of the permits.

## Quotes

> "Clearly, they are not because they are outside the jurisdiction of the United States." (260 F. Supp. 2d at 1013)
> "Accordingly, defendants correctly did not include the power plants themselves when defining the scope of the proposed action." (260 F. Supp. 2d at 1013)
> "Because the EBC turbine and the BCP transmission line are two links in the same chain, the emissions resulting from the operation of the EBC turbine are “effects” of the BCP transmission line that must be analyzed under NEPA." (260 F. Supp. 2d at 1017)
> "Additionally, because the record makes clear that the EAX export turbine has an alternative to the BCP line to export its power, the BCP line cannot be considered the but-for cause of the EAX export turbine’s operation." (260 F. Supp. 2d at 1017)

## Treatment

2026-09-24: CourtListener lists the case (also 2003 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 9333, 2003 WL 21037927); the cited-by count did not display in the search snippet.
2026-09-24: status set to questioned on our own reading. Its effects holding used a but-for test. Public Citizen, decided about a year later, held that but-for causation is insufficient and that an agency without authority to prevent an effect is not its legally relevant cause (541 U.S. at 767, 770). Seven County adds that an agency need not assess separate projects because they would not occur but for the project at hand, especially projects outside its regulatory authority (605 U.S. at 188, 190). A foreign power plant outside DOE's authority fits that description. The scope holding (plants not part of the federal action) is consistent with both.
