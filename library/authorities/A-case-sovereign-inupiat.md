## What it is

Ninth Circuit decision (R. Nelson, J., joined by Forrest, J.; R. Nelson, J., also concurring; Sanchez, J., concurring in part and dissenting in part) on BLM's 2023 approval of ConocoPhillips's Willow oil project in the National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska. Decided two weeks after Seven County, which it cites by slip opinion.

## Operative text or holding

On alternatives, the majority held that BLM's anti-segmentation rationale reasonably supported limiting action alternatives to those allowing full field development, because partial development would invite later permit applications and piecemeal review. Citing Delaware Riverkeeper and Seven County's deference principle, it found no abuse of discretion. But BLM's final chosen alternative did not fully meet that standard and BLM gave no reasoned explanation for the departure, so the approval was arbitrary and capricious under the APA. BLM's downstream emissions analysis complied with NEPA.

## Quotes

> "It is reasonable to think that a piecemeal development approach “fails to address the true scope and impact” of ConocoPhillips’s activities" (slip op. at 27)
> "Given the legitimate concerns associated with segmentation, we cannot say that BLM abused its discretion in using the full field development standard to avoid the risks of piecemeal development in the NPR-A." (slip op. at 29)
> "The anti-segmentation rationale is legally and logically untenable." (slip op. at 79, Sanchez, J., concurring in part and dissenting in part)

## Notes

- Use with care: this case uses anti-segmentation reasoning to support an agency's broader scope, not to require one. It shows the Ninth Circuit, after Seven County, still treating segmentation risk as a legitimate agency concern and citing Delaware Riverkeeper (753 F.3d at 1313) approvingly.
- The dissent's view (slip op. at 79 to 80) is that NEPA does not require agencies to ignore partial development alternatives, and that "Anti-segmentation seems to boil down to a plea of convenience".
- Cites Seven County as "Seven County Infrastructure Coal. v. Eagle County, No. 23-975, slip op. at 6 (U.S. May 29, 2025)" and as "Eagle County, slip op. at 12, 15".
- The PDF running head reads "CTR. FOR BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY V. BUREAU OF LAND MGMT."; the PDF file is filed under No. 23-3627.
