## What it is

The guidance, current and past, on EAs prepared by applicants, by contractors the applicant directs, or by contractors the bureau directs and the applicant pays for, and how that fits with BLM ROW cost recovery. The current Department-level guidance is Appendix 1, section 9 of the DOI NEPA Handbook, which implements 43 C.F.R. 46.105 and 46.107 (carded at A-cfr-43-46). The BLM-specific pieces are 43 C.F.R. 2804.19(c) for Category 6 cost recovery agreements and the 2008 BLM NEPA Handbook, section 13.5.

## Operative text or holding

- Two models. In the applicant-prepared model, the applicant prepares the EA or directs its own contractor. In "third party contracting", a bureau-directed contractor is funded by the applicant. The Handbook treats these as different arrangements.
- In either model the bureau stays responsible for accuracy, scope and content, must independently evaluate and approve the analysis, and may request more information, revise methods, or decline to use the document.
- Best practices: early coordination, a kick-off meeting, and a written request within 60 days for any added information. The Handbook recommends an MOU or other agreement setting out roles, schedule, deliverables, quality standards, a certification that all supporting studies will be provided, and a professional integrity statement.
- BLM ROW regulations: for Category 6 (more than 64 hours, or an EIS), BLM and the applicant sign a written cost recovery agreement. BLM keeps the option to prepare any environmental document. If BLM lets the applicant prepare documents or studies, the work must follow BLM standards, may be covered by a written agreement, and BLM makes the final determinations.
- BLM's 2008 NEPA Handbook (H-1790-1, sec. 13.5) recommends an MOU for third-party contracting providing that the applicant bears all contractor costs. BLM IM 2006-011 required an MOU for EISs and "major" EAs, but it expired on 09/30/2007.

## Quotes

> "Bureaus remain responsible for the accuracy, scope, and content of the EA or EIS and must independently evaluate and approve each such analysis before the bureau may rely on it." (DOI NEPA Handbook, App. 1, sec. 9, p. 47)
> "Applicant or applicant-directed preparation of an EA or EIS is thus different from circumstances where a bureau uses a bureau-directed contractor who may be funded by the applicant (an arrangement often referred to as “third party contracting”)." (App. 1, sec. 9, p. 48)
> "It is recommended that bureaus develop Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs) or other agreements with applicants and applicant-directed contractors to outline roles and responsibilities for all entities involved in the project." (App. 1, sec. 9, p. 48)
> "Within 60 days of the initial review or meeting, the bureau should identify in writing any additional information needed from the applicant or applicant-directed contractor to initiate the NEPA process." (App. 1, sec. 9, p. 48)
> "BLM retains the option to prepare any environmental documents related to your application. If BLM allows you to prepare any environmental documents and conduct any studies that BLM needs to process your application, you must do the work following BLM standards." (43 C.F.R. 2804.19(c))
> "BLM will make the final determinations and conclusions arising from such work." (43 C.F.R. 2804.19(c))
> "The BLM may permit an applicant to prepare the EA." (H-1790-1, sec. 13.5, p. 124)
