## What it is

The FAA rules that require advance notice (FAA Form 7460-1) of construction or alteration that may affect navigable airspace. The FAA uses the notice to decide whether a structure is a hazard to air navigation and what marking and lighting it needs. DoD reviews these filings through its Clearinghouse (see A-cfr-32-211). `decided` is set to the 2010 Part 77 rewrite's effective date, which was not verified in this session.

## Operative text or holding

- Notice is required (77.9) for any of the following:
  - (a) anything more than 200 ft AGL at its site;
  - (b) anything that penetrates an imaginary surface sloping 100:1 for 20,000 ft from the nearest runway of a qualifying airport whose longest runway is more than 3,200 ft. The slope is 50:1 for 10,000 ft for shorter runways and 25:1 for 5,000 ft from heliports;
  - (c) roads and railroads whose height, adjusted for traversing vehicles, would exceed (a) or (b);
  - (d) construction on a qualifying airport.
- Qualifying airports include public use airports, airports "operated by a Federal agency or the DOD", and airports with an FAA-approved instrument approach (77.9(d)).
- Timing (77.7(b)): file at least 45 days before the earlier of construction start or the date an application for a construction permit is filed.
- Exceptions (77.9(e)) cover shielded objects in congested areas, certain navigation aids, and antennas 20 ft or shorter.
- A supplemental notice is required for structures over 200 ft AGL or when requested (77.11).

## Quotes

> "Any construction or alteration that is more than 200 ft. AGL at its site." (14 CFR 77.9(a))
> "100 to 1 for a horizontal distance of 20,000 ft. from the nearest point of the nearest runway of each airport described in paragraph (d) of this section with its longest runway more than 3,200 ft. in actual length, excluding heliports." (14 CFR 77.9(b)(1))
> "An airport operated by a Federal agency or the DOD." (14 CFR 77.9(d)(3))
> "You must submit this form at least 45 days before the start date of the proposed construction or alteration or the date an application for a construction permit is filed, whichever is earliest." (14 CFR 77.7(b))

## Notes

- Hazen: NAS Fallon is a DoD-operated airport, so 77.9(b) surfaces apply around its runways. The distance from the Hazen site to the NAS Fallon runways was not measured. Separately, any structure over 200 ft AGL anywhere triggers notice. That could include gas plant stacks, tall transmission structures, geothermal or cooling structures, and data center stacks or towers. A 7460-1 filing is the formal route into DoD review under 49 U.S.C. 44718 and 10 U.S.C. 183a.
- Jean: the Jean Sport Aviation Center airport is in Jean. Its public-use status, runway length and distance from the substation and line were not verified. If it qualifies, transmission structures and the substation within the 77.9(b) surfaces need notice even well below 200 ft. Check the FAA Notice Criteria Tool. Cranes used during construction also count as temporary structures that may need notice; this was not researched.
- Local ordinances may add airport zoning requirements. Not researched.
