## What it is

The Board affirmed a BLM Taos Field Office EA/FONSI and decision granting rights-of-way for access roads and utility lines (4.67 miles, new and existing) across public land to private inholdings, and denied a motion to dismiss for lack of standing. One judge dissented.

## Operative text or holding

- Standing (then 43 C.F.R. 4.410): neighbors who use the access road and hold the ROW for it showed a legally cognizable interest likely to be injured by more traffic, so the appeal was not dismissed.
- NEPA scope: BLM properly limited its EA to the ROWs and treated private development as outside its jurisdiction. Indirect effects of private development must be considered only if they are caused by the ROW and reasonably foreseeable. Here development was speculative (water and zoning) and would happen, if at all, whether or not BLM granted the ROWs. The EA still discussed likely development, and the Board found that enough.
- An EA with a FONSI for a linear ROW is upheld where BLM took a hard look and the appellants did not show with objective proof a substantial environmental question BLM missed.

## Quotes

> "A party challenging a BLM decision to issue a right-of-way may show adverse effect through evidence of use of the lands in question." (174 IBLA at 93, headnote 1)
> "BLM properly authorizes rights-of-way for access roads and utility lines across public lands to private lands, absent preparation of an EIS" (174 IBLA at 94, headnote 3)
> "In this case, BLM rightly states that its jurisdiction extends to whether to approve the Proposed Action, but that it has no control over whether and to what extent the private properties will be developed." (174 IBLA at 110)
> "Development of those lands will materialize, if at all, independent of whether BLM issues the disputed ROWs." (174 IBLA at 110)

## Notes

- Pins: reporter pages come from the footers in the OHA PDF. The source file's [[page N]] markers are PDF pages.
- Standing rule now at 43 C.F.R. 4.402 and 4.401 (rules effective Feb. 10, 2025). See A-ibla-owyhee-county-standing.
- The indirect effects analysis applies the rescinded CEQ definition (40 C.F.R. 1508.8(b)) and James Shaw, 130 IBLA 105 (1994) (not carded). Under Seven County (A-case-seven-county), BLM's position on private development is, if anything, stronger.
- Relevance: the facts are close to Hazen Application 1 and FMDP: access roads and utility lines on public land serving private land, with the private development outside the agency's control.
- Dissent (Judge Hemmer) would have remanded over the range of alternatives and FLPMA section 501(b). It could be cited against an EA with a thin alternatives analysis.
