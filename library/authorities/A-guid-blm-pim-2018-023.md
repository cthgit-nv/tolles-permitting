## What it is

A BLM Washington Office permanent IM that amended BLM NEPA Handbook H-1790-1 (sections 6.5.2.1 and 7.3 and the glossary) to say that connected actions are limited to Federal actions that are currently proposed. It exists and is still posted on blm.gov.

## Operative text or holding

A non-Federal action is not a "connected action", even when it is closely related to a BLM action. If BLM decision-making can prevent or modify the non-Federal action or its effects, those effects are indirect effects of the BLM action and count toward significance. If BLM cannot prevent or modify them, they may still belong in the cumulative effects analysis. The PIM's example is a ROW for access to timber harvest on private land. The harvest is not a connected action, and whether its effects count depends on whether BLM's decision could prevent or modify it, for example whether the owner has other reasonable access. A connected action proposed by another Federal agency may be analyzed in the same document, or BLM must at least describe it and its relationship to the BLM action.

## Quotes

> "Connected actions are limited to Federal actions that are currently proposed (ripe for decision)." (PIM 2018-023, revising H-1790-1 sec. 6.5.2.1)
> "Therefore, a non-Federal action, even if “closely related” to a proposed BLM action, will not be a connected action pursuant to the Council on Environmental Quality regulations, because connected actions are limited to Federal actions." (PIM 2018-023, revising H-1790-1 sec. 6.5.2.1)
> "Rather, if the non-Federal action or its effects can be prevented or modified by BLM decision-making, then the effects of the non-Federal action are properly considered indirect effects of the BLM action and must be analyzed as effects of the BLM action" (PIM 2018-023, revising H-1790-1 sec. 6.5.2.1)
> "At a minimum, you must demonstrate that you have considered the connected action in the NEPA document for the proposed action (40 CFR 1508.25)." (PIM 2018-023, revising H-1790-1 sec. 6.5.2.1)

## Treatment

2026-09-24: Page live at blm.gov/policy/pim-2018-023. No rescission or superseding IM found on BLM's IM and PIM listings (pages 0 to 3 checked, FY2023 to FY2026). Status set to questioned, not superseded, because (1) it applies CEQ regulations (40 CFR 1508.25, 1508.7, 1508.8) that CEQ removed in 2025 (see A-fr-90-10610), and (2) it amends H-1790-1, which predates the February 2026 DOI NEPA Handbook. The Handbook's own definition of connected action (sec. 6.1(g), "a separate Federal action within the authority of a Federal agency") reaches the same Federal-only result, so the core point survives. ## Notes

- Memo check: the memo's description ("connected actions limited to federal actions") is accurate.
- The PIM cites Big Bend Conservation Alliance v. FERC (D.C. Cir. 2018), Sierra Club v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 803 F.3d 31 (D.C. Cir. 2015), and Sierra Club v. BLM, 786 F.3d 1219 (9th Cir. 2015) (wind project on private land and BLM access ROW not connected). Those are not carded here.
- Use with care: the indirect effects part of the PIM rests on the old CEQ definition of indirect effects. After Seven County (see A-case-seven-county), the stronger cite for limiting effects of other projects is that case, not this PIM.
- For FMDP, the Great Basin Gas Transmission pipeline is a Federal (FERC) action, so under the PIM's logic it could be a connected action if the tests are met. The PIM does not answer that, and the IBLA answer on shared corridors is in A-ibla-cbd-lincoln-county-row.
