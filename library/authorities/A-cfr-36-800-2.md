## What it is

The ACHP regulation that defines the role of Indian tribes in the NHPA Section 106 process. The agency official must consult any tribe that attaches religious and cultural significance to historic properties the undertaking may affect, wherever those properties are located. `decided` is set to the 2004 amendment date, which was not verified in this session. The eCFR section note was not captured.

## Operative text or holding

- On tribal lands: consult the THPO in place of the SHPO, or, for tribes without a THPO, a tribal designee in addition to the SHPO (800.2(c)(2)(i)).
- Off tribal lands: the agency must consult any tribe that attaches religious and cultural significance to affected historic properties, and that tribe is a consulting party (800.2(c)(2)(ii)).
  - Consultation must give the tribe a reasonable opportunity to identify concerns, advise on identification and evaluation (including properties of traditional religious and cultural importance), state its views on effects, and take part in resolving adverse effects.
  - The agency must make "a reasonable and good faith effort" to identify the tribes to consult.
  - Consultation should begin early and be government-to-government.
- Applicants: the agency may authorize applicants to start consultation with the SHPO/THPO, but it remains responsible for findings and for government-to-government relationships with tribes (800.2(c)(4)).

## Quotes

> "This requirement applies regardless of the location of the historic property. Such Indian tribe or Native Hawaiian organization shall be a consulting party." (36 CFR 800.2(c)(2)(ii))
> "It is the responsibility of the agency official to make a reasonable and good faith effort to identify Indian tribes and Native Hawaiian organizations that shall be consulted in the section 106 process." (36 CFR 800.2(c)(2)(ii)(A))
> "Consultation with an Indian tribe must recognize the government-to-government relationship between the Federal Government and Indian tribes." (36 CFR 800.2(c)(2)(ii)(C))
> "Federal agencies that provide authorizations to applicants remain responsible for their government-to-government relationships with Indian tribes." (36 CFR 800.2(c)(4))
