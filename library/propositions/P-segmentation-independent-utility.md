## Why it matters to us

This is the core risk in the Hazen two-application structure. Seven County's "no
authority" rationale does not apply: Reclamation decides Application 1 and part of
Application 2, and BLM and Reclamation together decide both. The question is whether the
crossings and Hart Lane road are one project with the FMDP corridor.

## Best supporting authority

- Save the Sound (D.C. Cir. July 2026, persuasive): projects are separate where they do
  not overlap temporally, have substantial independent value, and neither requires the
  other to go forward (slip op. 8 to 10). Note: the court tied these factors to the old
  CEQ rule and its own precedent; their footing after CEQ's rescission is untested.
- Delaware Riverkeeper (D.C. Cir. 2014): the test in reverse. Projects were one because
  of interrelatedness, temporal overlap, functional and financial interdependence, and
  no logical termini. Useful as the list of facts we must not have.
- Seven County: substantial deference to the agency's definition of the project.

## Best adverse authority and the answer to it

- **Thomas v. Peterson (9th Cir. 1985, binding, questioned on other grounds).** A road and
  the timber sales it served were connected actions: the sales could not proceed without
  the road, and the road would not be built but for the sales. This is the closest
  analogy an opponent has: "the access road and crossings would not be built but for the
  energy park the transmission line serves." Answer: (1) the Hart Lane road and crossings
  serve CHIP parcel access and utilities (water, wastewater, fiber, MV distribution)
  whether or not FMDP is built; (2) FMDP does not require Application 1 to proceed, since
  its access uses the NV Energy road and the shared GBGT corridor; (3) Thomas applied the
  CEQ connected-actions regulation, now rescinded, and Seven County directs deference.
  The answer fails if any Application 1 facility exists only to serve FMDP (see
  `Q-20260924-overhead-line-scope`).
- **Sovereign Inupiat (9th Cir. June 2025, binding).** Upheld BLM choosing a broad,
  anti-segmentation scope. Answer: it upholds agency discretion to go broad; it does not
  require breadth. But it shows a Ninth Circuit panel receptive to segmentation concerns,
  and it tells us BLM can defend drawing the line wide if it wants to.

## Limits

- Status is contested because the governing test after CEQ's rescission is unsettled in
  the Ninth Circuit, and the closest binding analogy (Thomas) cuts against us on its facts.
- Our application of it to Hazen is **advocacy** until counsel weighs in.
- Record discipline decides this in practice: purpose statements, timing, and whether any
  Hazen document calls Application 1 a phase.
