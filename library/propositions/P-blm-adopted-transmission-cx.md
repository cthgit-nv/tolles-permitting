## Why it matters to us

**Denim: ruled out (2026-09-24).** The substation is about 50 to 60 acres (denim facts F4),
far over the roughly 10-acre limit; the 1.2-mile road likely exceeds the 1-mile new-road
limit. The 1.25-mile 230 kV line alone would fit, but splitting it from the substation
would be segmentation. **FMDP**: the Aug 2026 plan had about 3.8
miles of line on BLM land and about 1.9 on Reclamation land (fmdp facts F3), each under 10
miles, though the whole line is about 11 miles.

## Best supporting authority

Handbook App. 2, 16.1(E)(20), adopted by BLM at 90 FR 42432, with BLM's stated intended
use: "construction of 10-mile transmission lines that do not authorize more than a
125-acre ROW and construction of 10-acre electric power substations." Fiber on
transmission facilities or in existing ROWs: 16.1(E)(3), (E)(12).

## Best adverse authority and the answer to it

- No BLM Nevada decision applying these adopted CEs was found (research log). In 2026 the
  Sierra Front FO still used an EA for a new 345 kV ROW (A-prec-blm-silver-springs-345kv-2026).
  Answer: agency practice lags the rule; the question is whether the field office will use it.
- **Segmentation.** Applying a 10-mile CX to the BLM portion of an 11-mile line, while
  Reclamation reviews the rest, invites the argument that the line was split to fit the
  limit. The limits speak of the line, not the federal share; this is a real risk.
- **Extraordinary circumstances.** Desert tortoise habitat (Denim) and military,
  cultural or controversy issues (Hazen) can defeat the CX.
- **"Generally"** in the limits gives the agency discretion both ways.

## Limits

Open. Needs footprint facts (Denim: `Q-20260924-route-and-footprint`), a counsel view, and
