13a. Describe other alternative locations considered.
Alternatives evaluated for connecting the diagonally adjacent private CHIP parcels included the selected alternative consisting of corner crossings. Alternative 2 consists of
parallel crossings where a road or utility would cross a BOR parcel from north to south or east to west parallel to the parcel boundary creating a corridor through the BOR parcel.
Alternative 3 consists of acquiring or exchanging the intervening Reclamation-administered sections.

b. Why were these alternatives not selected?
Alternative 2 was not selected as creating a corridor across BOR land from east to west or north to south, may adversely affect the ability of BOR to use the remaining areas of the parcel.
Acquisition or exchange would not meet the development schedule. The selected configuration, a road and utility corridor approximately 500 feet wide crossing directly over each section corner,
confines the permanent federal use for the crossings to approximately 20.1 acres; The configuration remains subject to confirmation against the engineering test fit; if additional width or a different
configuration is required, the requested crossing areas will be revised before filing.

c. Give explanation as to why it is necessary to use or occupy Federal assets (lands or buildings).
The land surrounding CHIP is a checkerboard in which even-numbered sections are Reclamation-administered and odd-numbered sections are private. Diagonally adjacent
private parcels meet only at a shared section corner, so no continuous corridor avoids Reclamation land. A common-law corner-crossing theory does not authorize construction
or occupancy of roads or utilities on Reclamation land, and because a right-of-way granted by the BLM under Title V of FLPMA does not authorize use of land withdrawn for
Reclamation purposes, a separate use authorization under 43 CFR Part 429 is necessary.
14. List authorizations and pending applications filed for similar projects which may provide information to the authorizing agency. (Specify number,
date, code, or name)
No prior or pending authorization has been issued or applied for from the Bureau of Reclamation for this Project.




15. Provide statement of need for project, including the economic feasibility and items such as: (a) cost of proposal (construction, operation,
and maintenance); (b) estimated cost of next best alternative; and (c) expected public benefits.
The Project is needed to provide continuous, legally authorized surface access and utility connectivity between privately held parcels of the proposed Churchill Hazen Industrial Park that meet only at shared section
corners. Without an authorized crossing at each corner the parcels cannot be connected by road or utility line, and any future development cannot be built or operated. (a) Cost of proposal: to be provided when
engineering is complete. (b) Cost of next best alternative: to be provided. (c) Expected public benefits: The road improvements will provide connectivity between project parcels and facilitate the installation, operation,
and maintenance of project utilities, including electrical, communications, and related infrastructure.


16. Describe probable effects on the population in the area, including the social and economic aspects, and the rural lifestyles.
The Project area is sparsely populated open rangeland, irrigated agriculture, and industrial land; no residential displacement is proposed. Future NEPA compliance, may evaluate impacts near
the project area beyond the direct effects of the proposed action, including related infrastructure and facilities such as CHIP facilities, where appropriate. Effects on the local population are
expected to be primarily beneficial and economic. Construction would generate temporary employment and local expenditure, and most construction workers are expected to commute from
Fernley, Fallon, Silver Springs, and the greater Reno-Sparks area. Temporary construction traffic and localized access restrictions would occur within active work areas.

17. Describe likely environmental effects that the proposed project will have on: (a) air quality; (b) visual impact; (c) surface and ground water quality and
quantity; (d) the control or structural change on any stream or other body of water; (e) existing noise levels; and (f) the surface of the land, including
vegetation, permafrost, soil, and soil stability; and, (g) historic or archaeological resources or properties.
(a) Air quality: temporary fugitive dust and equipment exhaust during clearing, grading, and trenching; Churchill County is in attainment or unclassifiable for all NAAQS; dust control and, if the threshold is exceeded, a Nevada Surface Area Disturbance permit. (b)
Visual: small, ground-level features in developed open basin terrain. (c) Surface and ground water: no Newlands Project conveyance lies within a crossing; National Hydrography Dataset and National Wetlands Inventory data show two unnamed ephemeral
streams and three riverine features in Crossings 2 and 3; an aquatic resources delineation would determine whether Clean Water Act Section 404 authorization is required. (d) No control of, or structural change to, any water body is proposed; runoff would not be
directed toward a Reclamation embankment. (e) Noise: temporary construction noise only. (f) Land surface: approximately 20.1 acres in the four crossings; no grazing allotment affected; reclamation and weed control proposed. (g) Historic and archaeological
resources may be found within the corner crossings. A Section 106 review, Class I records search, Class III inventory, or Indian Trust Assets assessment will be conducted, if required


18. Describe the probable effects that the proposed project will have on (a) populations of fish, plant life, wildlife, and marine life, including threatened and
endangered species; and (b) marine mammals, including hunting, capturing, collecting, or killing these animals.
No designated critical habitat for federally listed species occurs within the Project area, and the area does not contain greater sage-grouse Priority, General, or Other Habitat Management Areas mapped under the
2025 Nevada and Northeastern California Approved Resource Management Plan Amendment. Habitat is Great Basin salt desert shrub supporting small mammals, reptiles, raptors, and passerine birds. Federally
listed, proposed, and candidate species were identified through the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Information for Planning and Consultation system, and consultation under Section 7 of the Endangered Species
Act may not be required based on findings.

19. State whether any hazardous material, as defined in this paragraph, would be used, produced, transported or stored on or in a federal building or federal lands or would be used
in connection with the proposed use or occupancy. “Hazardous material” shall mean (a) any hazardous substance under section 101(14) of the Comprehensive Environmental
Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA), 42 U.S.C. § 9601(14); (b) any pollutant or contaminant under section 101(33) of CERCLA, 42 U.S.C. § 9601(33); (c) any
petroleum product or its derivative, including fuel oil, and waste oils; and (d) any hazardous substance, extremely hazardous substance, toxic substance, hazardous waste, ignitable,
reactive or corrosive materials, pollutant, contaminant, element, compound, mixture, solution or substance that may pose a present or potential hazard to human health or the
environment under any applicable environmental laws. The holder shall not store any hazardous materials at the site without prior written approval from the authorized officer. This
approval shall not be unreasonably withheld. If the authorized officer provides approval, this permit shall include (or in the case of approval provided after this permit is issued, shall
be amended to include) specific terms addressing the storage of hazardous materials, including the specific type of materials to be stored, the volume, the type of storage, and a spill
plan. Such terms shall be proposed by the holder and are subject to approval by the authorized officer.

Yes. Hazardous materials as defined in this paragraph would be used, transported, and temporarily stored on federal lands during construction, operation, and maintenance. Anticipated
materials include diesel fuel and gasoline; motor oil, gear oil, and lubricating grease; hydraulic fluid; antifreeze and coolant; transmission and brake fluid; solvents, degreasers, and parts
cleaners; paints, primers, coatings, and thinners; concrete, grout, and concrete admixtures; compressed gases for welding and cutting; batteries; herbicides applied under the applicable
federal permit; and dust palliatives and soil stabilizers. No long-term storage of hazardous materials is proposed within the requested authorization boundary, and no transformer
insulating oil or sulfur hexafluoride would be present on Reclamation-administered land under this application. Refueling, equipment servicing, and hazardous material storage would not
occur within a Reclamation facility right-of-way. No hazardous material would be stored at the site without prior written approval of the authorized officer, and the Applicant will propose
specific storage terms, volumes, and a spill plan for approval. All hazardous materials would be handled, stored, and disposed of in accordance with applicable federal, state, and local
requirements, including 40 CFR Part 262. No nuclear or byproduct materials would be used. See Plan of Development, Sections 7 and 10.
