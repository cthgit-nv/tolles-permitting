# Q-20260924-existing-gravel-road-status: What is the legal status of the existing gravel road, and does the Hart Lane road follow it?

- **Raised:** 2026-09-24 by Cory (can we keep using it for construction?)
- **Project:** CHIP (Application 1)
- **Sensitivity:** INTERNAL
- **Blocking?** Yes for construction access; it also shapes the CX argument.
- **Who can answer:** Title company or surveyor; Churchill County Public Works; LBAO realty; TCID

## Context

Under 43 C.F.R. 429.3, possession or occupancy of Reclamation land, including
transportation infrastructure, requires a use authorization. The only general exemptions
are individual non-commercial use (429.4(a)) and authorized O&M by water user
organizations (429.4(c)(6)). Construction traffic is commercial use. Unauthorized use
exposes us to back fees, interest and costs, and Reclamation "may deny future use
applications by you because of this behavior" (429.33(a), (d)), which would put
Application 1 at risk.

Three possibilities, each with a different answer:
1. **County road with a recorded right-of-way or easement across Reclamation land.** Public
   road; construction traffic is allowed subject to county rules (weight limits, haul
   agreement, maintenance). No Part 429 authorization needed for the traffic.
2. **TCID operation and maintenance road.** Authorized for O&M only; construction use needs
   Reclamation authorization and TCID concurrence.
3. **Road on Reclamation land with no recorded authorization** (long use does not create a
   right against the United States). Needs a use authorization before construction use.

Precedent for case 2 or 3: LBAO licensed non-exclusive use of an existing gravel road on
Reclamation land (60 feet wide, about 4,699 feet) under a categorical exclusion, with
future utilities allowed (Marburg Technology Road License, LO-2022-1023; research log
2026-09-24-seed-agency-ibla).

Also: if the Hart Lane road would pave the existing gravel alignment, most of its
disturbance is on previously disturbed land, which strengthens D(8) prong (a)
(`P-reclamation-cx-d8-fit`; Reclamation's 2025 preamble names ground disturbance on
previously undisturbed land as a factor).

**Update 2026-09-25 (Cory):** the Hart Lane road follows the existing gravel road and stops at the CHIP property line (facts F12). Legal status of the gravel road (county right-of-way or not) is still open.
