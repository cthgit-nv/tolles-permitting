## Decision

**Application 1 (CHIP), Bureau of Reclamation only, 43 C.F.R. Part 429.** Limited section
corner crossings on Newlands Project parcels, carrying roads, water, wastewater,
overhead lines, medium-voltage electric, fiber optic, and similar utilities. Plus a paved
access road of about 2,750 feet connecting Hart Lane to the westernmost CHIP section.
The road continues an existing county road; the new portion is entirely on Reclamation
land.

**Application 2 (FMDP), the broader application.** Linear infrastructure only:
transmission lines, access roads, and related facilities, under BLM FLPMA Title V
(and Reclamation for any segment on Reclamation land; see open question
`projects/fmdp/questions/open/Q-20260924-fmdp-reclamation-segments.md`). The corridor is
planned to share alignment and access with the Great Basin Gas Transmission pipeline (to
be built) and the existing NV Energy road and transmission line. All solar, battery
storage, geothermal and other generation is on private land and is not part of either
federal application.

## Why

Application 1 is CHIP's critical path (parcel-to-parcel access and utilities across the
checkerboard) and may qualify for a Reclamation categorical exclusion. Keeping the
corridor out of it keeps Application 1 small and fast.

## Rejected

One combined submission with the Reclamation crossings, BLM crossings and the transmission
corridor in a single NEPA document (the Aug 14 risk memo structure, Q2 option one).
Rejected because it ties the crossings to the corridor's EA schedule.

## Reverse this if

- Counsel concludes Application 1 lacks independent utility from Application 2, so
  splitting them is impermissible segmentation.
- Reclamation declines a categorical exclusion for the road and full utility bundle and
  requires an EA anyway, removing the schedule benefit.
- Application 1's "overhead" facilities turn out to include segments of the FMDP
  transmission line (see `projects/chip/questions/open/Q-20260924-overhead-line-scope.md`).
